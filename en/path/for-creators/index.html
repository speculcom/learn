The Image / Video / Audio Creator's Route

StarterNo background needed: build the concepts first

about 40 minutes

Study generation across text, image, video and sound, including failures, provenance and publication conditions. Aim to define acceptance checks.

Read in this order
  1. Generative AI
  2. Text-to-Image Generation
  3. Video Generation
  4. TTS, Text-to-Speech
  5. Multimodal
  6. Watermarks and Content Credentials
  7. Generative AI Compliance Duties
  8. Privacy & Compliance
Checkpoint
  1. Name one main failure mode for text-to-image, video generation and speech synthesis each
  2. Given a generation tool, check commercial licensing and training-data provenance before judging output
  3. Know the labelling duties for publishing AI-generated content and which assets cannot be used commercially
  4. Why does text in generated images often fail, and how do you check it?
  5. Where do you check temporal consistency in generated video?
  6. Beyond sounding pleasant, what should speech synthesis acceptance check?
  7. Why record input provenance and output licence separately in multimodal generation?
  8. What determines the labelling duty for AI-generated content?
  9. How do you assess training-data provenance risk for a commercial job?
  10. Compress this route into an acceptance checklist with at least five items.

Try answering before opening the reference. Revisit the relevant step above if needed. These answers highlight key ideas; you do not need to repeat the wording.

Question 1 · Reference answer

Check local structure and text in images, continuity across video frames, and pronunciation, pacing and identity authorization in speech. Use explicit checks rather than overall polish.

Revisit this concept

Question 2 · Reference answer

Read terms for the actual product, tier and asset type. Check input rights, intended output use and training use separately. Permission to publish does not clear every third-party right.

Revisit this concept

Question 3 · Reference answer

Check current labeling rules for the publication region and platform. Record sources and permissions for fonts, music, likenesses and references separately. Replace or withhold unresolved assets.

Revisit this concept

Question 4 · Reference answer

Image models generate in pixel or latent space and are weak at exact glyphs and layout; small text, long words, numbers and brand names fail most. Zoom in and read every character, then fix typography afterwards; thumbnails are not enough.

Revisit this concept

Question 5 · Reference answer

Check whether the subject’s shape and identity drift across frames, and whether background and lighting jump during camera motion. Then inspect hands, text and fast action, where breaks show first.

Revisit this concept

Question 6 · Reference answer

Check pronunciation (heteronyms, proper nouns), numbers and units, pauses and stress, emotional fit, and authorisation for voice cloning. Pleasant is not publishable.

Revisit this concept

Question 7 · Reference answer

Input copyright, likeness and training-data provenance are separate from what the product terms allow you to do with output. A lawful input does not clear output for commercial use, or vice versa.

Revisit this concept

Question 8 · Reference answer

Check the official text in force for the publication region, platform rules and content type; deep synthesis, news, advertising and general creation differ. Record the document, date and applicable clause.

Revisit this concept

Question 9 · Reference answer

Check whether the terms state training-data provenance and indemnity, then whether inputs contain client-unauthorised brands, people or works. If unresolved, change tool, change asset or do not use the output.

Revisit this concept

Question 10 · Reference answer

Use text/numbers, cross-frame consistency, pronunciation and voice rights, asset provenance, commercial output licence and AI labelling. Name the checker and evidence for each; deliver only after all pass.
