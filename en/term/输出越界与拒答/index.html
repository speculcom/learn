Output Overreach and Refusal

Output Overreach and Refusal

  • Model capabilities
  • Stage 2 · Hands On
  • refs checked 2026-11-06
What problem it solves

See which requests a model will decline, which it will answer only partly, and when it does more than you asked for.

What it is
Refusal is the model declining to cooperate with a request. Overreach runs the other way: it does work you did not ask for, such as editing a file you had not meant to touch. OpenAI's Model Spec sorts the instructions a model must obey into platform, developer and user tiers, tells it to refuse neutrally and briefly, and allows a partial answer when the full request cannot be met. Refusal itself turns out to be a tunable behaviour. Arditi and colleagues reported in 2024 that removing one internal direction stopped models from refusing harmful requests, while adding that same direction produced refusals even on harmless ones. The same request may be refused by one product and answered by another. That is a difference in training objective and policy configuration, not a sign that your prompt was badly written.
Why it matters

When you get a refusal you need to know the next move: rephrase to recover a partial answer, or switch tools. When the model has done extra work you need to recognise it as overreach rather than helpfulness. Telling the two apart saves a lot of rework.
