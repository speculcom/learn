Data Residency and Cross-border Transfer

Data Residency and Cross-border Transfer

  • Data & retrieval
  • Stage 5 · Running It
  • refs checked 2026-11-06
What problem it solves

Separates "which region stores the data" from "on what legal basis may it leave that region".

What it is
Residency is about storage location and is a configuration decision. Cross-border transfer is about the lawful route and is a legal decision. Picking a region does not by itself give you a basis for exporting data. In the cloud, residency is usually a choice you make: AWS's data privacy FAQs state that the customer chooses the region where content is stored, and that AWS will not move or replicate content outside the chosen regions without the customer's agreement. On the EU side the mechanism is contractual: the European Commission publishes pre-approved Standard Contractual Clauses for transfers of personal data to third countries. On the China side the mechanism is a filed assessment. Article 4 of the Measures for Security Assessment of Outbound Data Transfers lists the situations that require a declaration, including providing important data abroad, and personal-information transfers by critical information infrastructure operators or processors of data on more than one million people. Self-hosting moves the question rather than removing it: prompts and logs stay local, but weights, update sources and any external service you call - vector store, monitoring, third-party APIs - may still sit in another jurisdiction.
Why it matters

A compliance review does not ask which vendor's model you use. It asks which jurisdiction the data reaches, on what legal route, and who signed for it. A screenshot of a region selector does not answer the second question.
