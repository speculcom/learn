AI Chronicle and Atlas

Getting into AI starts with who's who and what came first. Three columns here: a timeline of key models, a company map, and how the vocabulary changed. Every entry carries an official source.

Timeline of key models
  1. 2025-01

    DeepSeek-R1

    Reasoning ability trained via reinforcement learning, with the method open-sourced. DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning (arXiv 2501.12948) ↗

  2. 2024-12

    DeepSeek-V3

    A Chinese team's open-weight model approaching top closed models on benchmarks. DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report (arXiv 2412.19437) ↗

  3. 2024-04

    Llama 3

    Meta's open-weight family; its technical report follows in July. The Llama 3 Herd of Models (arXiv 2407.21783) ↗

  4. 2024-03

    Claude 3

    A three-tier product line; on the published evaluations of the time, the top tier came close to leading closed models. Introducing the Claude 3 family (Anthropic official) ↗

  5. 2023-12

    Gemini

    Google's multimodal model family, launched after DeepMind and Brain merged. Gemini 1 Technical Report (DeepMind official PDF) ↗

  6. 2023-10

    Mistral 7B

    A small model beats larger ones on benchmarks, open-weight. Mistral 7B (arXiv 2310.06825) ↗

  7. 2023-07

    Llama 2

    Meta releases an open-weight model that permits commercial use. Llama 2: Open Foundation and Fine-Tuned Chat Models (arXiv 2307.09288) ↗

  8. 2023-03

    Claude launches

    Anthropic's conversational model goes public, with safety alignment front and center. Claude (Anthropic official) ↗

  9. 2023-03

    GPT-4

    Multimodal input and stronger reasoning, published with an official technical report. GPT-4 Technical Report (arXiv 2303.08774) ↗

  10. 2023-02

    LLaMA

    Meta releases open-weight models; a large family of community derivatives follows. LLaMA: Open and Efficient Foundation Language Models (arXiv 2302.13971) ↗

  11. 2022-11

    ChatGPT launches

    Conversational AI reaches the public; "AI" starts defaulting to chatbots. Introducing ChatGPT (OpenAI official) ↗

  12. 2022-03

    InstructGPT

    Fine-tuning with human feedback (RLHF): models start following instructions. Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback (arXiv 2203.02155) ↗

  13. 2021-07

    Codex

    Models trained on code write code; the AI-coding path opens here. Evaluating Large Language Models Trained on Code (arXiv 2107.03374) ↗

  14. 2020-05

    GPT-3

    Few-shot learning: no training, just a few examples in the prompt. Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (arXiv 2005.14165) ↗

  15. 2019-02

    GPT-2

    OpenAI demonstrates publicly: scale up the model, general ability appears. Language Models are Unsupervised Multitask Learners (OpenAI official PDF) ↗

  16. 2018-10

    BERT

    Bidirectional encoding becomes the standard pre-training recipe of that period. BERT (arXiv 1810.04805) ↗

  17. 2018-06

    GPT-1

    OpenAI shows that pre-training then fine-tuning works for language models. Improving Language Understanding by Generative Pre-Training (OpenAI official PDF) ↗

  18. 2017-06

    Transformer paper

    "Attention Is All You Need" is published. Every mainstream large model today builds on this architecture. Attention Is All You Need (arXiv 1706.03762) ↗

How the vocabulary changed

  1. "Foundation model"

    Formally defined by a Stanford team in 2021: models trained on broad data and adaptable to many downstream tasks. On the Opportunities and Risks of Foundation Models (arXiv 2108.07258) ↗

  2. "Alignment"

    InstructGPT made training models to follow human feedback mainstream in 2022. Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback (arXiv 2203.02155) ↗

  3. "Hallucination"

    Models inventing plausible-sounding falsehoods; the term moved from researcher slang to common vocabulary. International AI Safety Report 2026, Extended Summary for Policymakers ↗

  4. "Agent"

    Once models could call tools, "AI that does things by itself" became common vocabulary. OpenAI Function Calling Guide (official) ↗

  5. "Reasoning model"

    Closed long-chain-of-thought models came first; DeepSeek-R1 open-sourced the reinforcement-learning recipe in 2025. DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning (arXiv 2501.12948) ↗
