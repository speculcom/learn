How to Put AI into an Existing Office Workflow

Guide · about 9 min · refs checked 2026-11-17 (reference links and dates only)

The biggest office-AI failure is full automation. Start with one high-frequency, well-ruled and reversible step, and let AI draft only.

How to
Pick the step: high frequency, clear rules, reversible errors; do not start with a cross-department process. Draw the current flow along four lines — input, approval, output and exception — and replace only one segment. Draft, not final: AI produces a review copy and a human keeps signing authority. Narrow permissions to read-only or a single-table write; never run automation on a personal account. Define failure paths: timeout, missing fields or refusal returns to a human; never skip silently. Log every run’s input, output and human edits; review the edit rate after three months. Use existing tools such as Power Automate, n8n or a multi-dimensional table before building a platform, and track time saved, rework and error rate together.

Example and interpretation

A teaching scenario, not a measured or executed site test.

Take one real workflow and write three sentences: which step AI does, who signs off, and who receives failures. If you cannot write them, do not launch.

How to check your practice

Explain the example in your own words, inspect input constraints, then verify against the source. Revisit concepts if you cannot explain why; supply missing dates, fields or permissions; withdraw unsupported conclusions.
