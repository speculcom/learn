How to Design a Human Review Step

Guide · about 7 min · refs checked 2026-11-06 (reference links and dates only)

Human review is not "add a person who clicks confirm"; it is a step where someone can see the error, can block it, and carries a named responsibility.

How to
Set the trigger conditions first: money, external publication, irreversible actions, or cases where the model flags its own uncertainty go to review; everything else should not consume human attention. Show reviewers comparison material, not just conclusions: source text beside the generated text, citation IDs, and the parts the model says it is unsure about. Hand over only a conclusion and review degrades into a rubber stamp. Define the available actions: edit, reject, escalate. If the workflow offers only an "approve" button, the rejection rate stays at zero and the metric carries no information. Guard against automation bias: long streaks of quick approvals mean people stopped looking. Sampling review quality (disagreement between reviewers, items later corrected) tells you more than sampling model output alone. Track three numbers per task type: review time, rejection rate, distribution of rejection reasons. Use them to work back to whether the prompt is vague or the acceptance standard is unclear. Worked example (fictional scenario): input is a model-written "summary of breach clauses in a contract" -> processing has the reviewer check three key statements against the source and mark "consistent / inconsistent / unsure", citing the paragraph -> the rule is: inconsistent gets rejected with the basis paragraph noted, unsure escalates to legal -> if two categories in a row come back inconsistent, next step is to default that task type back to human drafting and then fix the prompt and retrieval, rather than asking reviewers to check harder.

Example and interpretation

A teaching scenario, not a measured or executed site test.

Set contract-extraction review to three questions: does the conclusion match a clause, did it refuse when it should have, are amounts and dates copied right. Log who judged each item and on what rubric, not just pass or fail.

How to check your practice

Explain the example in your own words, inspect input constraints, then verify against the source. Revisit concepts if you cannot explain why; supply missing dates, fields or permissions; withdraw unsupported conclusions.
