Is Multi-GPU Interconnect Worth It?

Guide · about 8 min · refs checked 2026-11-19 (reference links and dates only)

More GPUs is not additive VRAM. Calculate communication, parallelism and real throughput before spending.

How to
Find the bottleneck first: not enough VRAM, not enough compute, or need more concurrency. Each has a different fix. Tensor parallelism eats interconnect bandwidth; pipeline parallelism eats VRAM and bubbles. Check NVLink, PCIe and Thunderbolt gaps first. Calculate effective throughput: not summed peak FLOPS, but end-to-end tokens/s and time to first token. Calculate total cost of ownership: cards, board, PSU, cooling, room, power, operations; not just card price. Small models at low concurrency are usually cheaper on one card; multi-GPU suits large models, long context and high concurrency. Rent before buying: run the real workload for a week on the same cloud configuration, then decide.

Example and interpretation

A teaching scenario, not a measured or executed site test.

Benchmark a 70B 4-bit model on one GPU for tokens/s and time-to-first-token, then on two GPUs with tensor parallelism. If two cards add only 20% throughput while doubling power, do not buy the second card; use a smaller model or stronger quantization.

How to check your practice

Explain the example in your own words, inspect input constraints, then verify against the source. Revisit concepts if you cannot explain why; supply missing dates, fields or permissions; withdraw unsupported conclusions.
