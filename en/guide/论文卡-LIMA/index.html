Paper Card - LIMA

Paper card · about 7 min · refs checked 2026-11-03 (reference links and dates only)

The 'less is more' alignment-data experiment: fine-tuning on just 1,000 curated examples. This card lays out what it set out to test, where the evidence sits, and what it does not claim.

What to take away
Research question: the abstract describes training LIMA (a 65B LLaMa model) "fine-tuned with the standard supervised loss on only 1,000 carefully curated prompts and responses", "without any reinforcement learning or human preference modeling" — the point is to measure the relative importance of the two training stages. Core evidence: Section 4's human evaluation (against GPT-4, Bard, DaVinci003) and Section 5's ablations on data diversity/quality/quantity — the abstract gives the controlled human-study number: "responses from LIMA are either equivalent or strictly preferred to GPT-4 in 43% of cases"; the conclusion line is "almost all knowledge in large language models is learned during pretraining". Author-stated limits: no standalone Limitations section (checked against the arXiv HTML version on 2026-10-30); Section 7's Discussion states two limits outright — the mental effort of building such examples is hard to scale, and "LIMA is not as robust as product-grade models" (an unlucky decode or an adversarial prompt often yields a weak response). What it does not claim: 43%/58%/65% are shares of "equivalent or strictly preferred" in a controlled human study — not "matches GPT-4 overall"; "1,000 examples suffice" is evidence about dialogue style, not about safety, factuality, or robustness — the very areas the authors admit are weak. How to read it: its value is turning the hypothesis "knowledge comes from pretraining, alignment data shapes style" into testable ablations; when citing it, separate "little data can align a model" from "little data can align everything".

Example and interpretation

A teaching scenario, not a measured or executed site test.

Separate “curated examples can change response style” from “little data solves everything.” Inspect human evaluations and ablations alongside robustness/safety limits. Preference proportions do not establish equal overall capability.

How to check your practice

Explain the example in your own words, inspect input constraints, then verify against the source. Revisit concepts if you cannot explain why; supply missing dates, fields or permissions; withdraw unsupported conclusions.
