How to Check Copyright Risk for AI-Generated Content

Guide · about 8 min · refs checked 2026-11-03 (reference links and dates only)

AI content copyright has two ends: whether the data fed in is lawful, and whether what comes out belongs to you. This guide checks both ends.

How to
Input side, check provenance: Chinese rules require "lawfully sourced data and foundation models". Input side, check exceptions: the EU Directive on Copyright in the Digital Single Market sets text-and-data-mining exceptions, with research and commercial terms differing. Output side, do not assume ownership: whether AI-generated content can be copyrighted differs by jurisdiction; check local practice first. Before commercial use, read the vendor terms: model terms of use differ on commercial use, competing products, and output ownership. Keep provenance records: where materials came from and what the training data was — the chain of provenance comes first in disputes. Litigation is ongoing: case law changes; for major commercial decisions consult legal counsel, not news headlines.

Example and interpretation

A teaching scenario, not a measured or executed site test.

Before publishing a generated poster, record reference-image rights, output terms, third-party elements and publication region separately. Commercial-use permission does not automatically resolve references, likenesses or copyrightability.

How to check your practice

Explain the example in your own words, inspect input constraints, then verify against the source. Revisit concepts if you cannot explain why; supply missing dates, fields or permissions; withdraw unsupported conclusions.
