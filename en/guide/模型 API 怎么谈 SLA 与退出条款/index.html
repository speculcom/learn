How to Negotiate Model API SLAs and Exit Terms

Guide · about 9 min · refs checked 2026-11-19 (reference links and dates only)

The trap in a model API is not price; it is availability, rate limits, data rights, and not being able to leave.

How to
Availability: write monthly uptime, incident response and credits; do not accept “best efforts”. Rate limits and quota: RPM, TPM, concurrency, bursts; say whether overage queues or errors, and put it in the contract. Data rights: who owns input and output, is it used for training, how long is it kept, can it be deleted; align with the privacy policy. Model changes: the vendor must give notice before changing the base or version; you keep the right to regression-test and terminate. Exit: export format, migration help, cache and backup deletion, final billing date. Price: unit price, tiers, minimum spend, cap on increases; do not look only at the first-month discount. Liability: boundaries and insurance for IP infringement, data breach and service interruption.

Example and interpretation

A teaching scenario, not a measured or executed site test.

Translate &quot;99.9% uptime&quot; into 43 minutes of allowed downtime per month, then ask whether throttling queues or errors and how credits are calculated. Add a clause requiring 30 days' notice before a base-model change plus a 30-day regression window.

How to check your practice

Explain the example in your own words, inspect input constraints, then verify against the source. Revisit concepts if you cannot explain why; supply missing dates, fields or permissions; withdraw unsupported conclusions.
