Paper Card - Chinchilla

Paper card · about 7 min · refs checked 2026-11-03 (reference links and dates only)

The calibration paper on 'how big should the model be, how much data should it see'. This card quotes the conclusion, states the evidence scale, and notes the extrapolation boundary the authors themselves flag.

What to take away
Research question (abstract): under a fixed compute budget, what is the optimal model size and token count; the finding is that current LLMs are "significantly undertrained" because the field scaled models while keeping data constant. Conclusion verbatim: "for compute-optimal training, the model size and the number of training tokens should be scaled equally: for every doubling of model size the number of training tokens should also be doubled". Evidence scale: 400+ models trained (70M to 16B+ parameters, 5B to 500B tokens) to fit the scaling law, then validated with the 70B Chinchilla, which "outperforms Gopher" (Section 4.2, verified). Author-flagged boundary (verified): Chinchilla trained on 4× more data than Gopher, and the authors warn train/test leakage may artificially boost results — even a calibration paper guards against contamination. What it does not claim: the law is fitted on ≤16B models and extrapolated — 'the rule holds past 70B' is extrapolation, not measurement; 'parameter count doesn't matter' is not the paper's claim either.

Example and interpretation

A teaching scenario, not a measured or executed site test.

Fix compute budget before comparing parameter/data allocation in two training plans. Model size alone does not establish efficiency; methods and the paper's scope also matter.

How to check your practice

Explain the example in your own words, inspect input constraints, then verify against the source. Revisit concepts if you cannot explain why; supply missing dates, fields or permissions; withdraw unsupported conclusions.
