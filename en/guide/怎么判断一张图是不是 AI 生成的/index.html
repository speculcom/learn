How to Tell Whether an Image Is AI-Generated

Guide · about 7 min · refs checked 2026-11-17 (reference links and dates only)

Looking at pixels is no longer enough. Defensible judgments rest on four evidence chains: source, original file, credentials and timeline.

How to
Check source and credentials: C2PA / Content Credentials can record capture and edits; no credential does not prove fake, and a credential still needs signature verification. Run a reverse image search and check publication dates; AI images often have no earlier origin. Look at visual cues: text, fingers, repeated textures, mirrored reflections and shadow direction — but model progress keeps invalidating them. Check noise and compression consistency: composites often show mismatched noise, repeated blocks or edge discontinuities. Use detectors only as aids: they are fragile under compression, re-editing and out-of-distribution images; a probability is not a verdict. The strongest evidence is the original file, capture chain and witnesses. Platforms should retain the uploaded original and processing logs. Conclude in three grades: proven fake, highly suspicious, cannot determine. Do not force a binary answer.

Example and interpretation

A teaching scenario, not a measured or executed site test.

For any image, record four things first: source, original file, credentials and timeline. If all four are missing, the conclusion is “cannot determine”, not “AI-generated”.

How to check your practice

Explain the example in your own words, inspect input constraints, then verify against the source. Revisit concepts if you cannot explain why; supply missing dates, fields or permissions; withdraw unsupported conclusions.
