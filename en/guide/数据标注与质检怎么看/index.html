How to Read Data Annotation and QA

Guide · about 8 min · refs checked 2026-11-03 (reference links and dates only)

"We used a million annotated rows" says nothing about quality. How to size up a training or eval dataset from three angles: who labeled it, how consistent they were, and how QA ran.

How to
Start with who did the labeling: crowdworkers, in-house staff, or domain experts — unit price is usually a good proxy for data quality. Check inter-annotator agreement: low agreement on the same batch means the guidelines themselves are ambiguous, so the noise is real. QA process beats label count: sampling rate, rework loop, and disagreement arbitration — miss any of the three and the scale number is hollow. "What counts as correct" must be written into the annotation guidelines: vague rules make annotators guess, and guessed data trains a wobbly model. Model-labeled data (synthetic data, AI feedback) is not exempt: it carries the labeling model's biases; at minimum add human spot-checks plus eval regression. Data problems surface in evals eventually: when model behavior gets weird, audit the latest batch's guideline changes before touching hyperparameters.

Example and interpretation

A teaching scenario, not a measured or executed site test.

If annotators disagree on “Good product, slow delivery,” inspect definitions and mixed-review rules. Preserve the disagreement, add examples and recheck a sample instead of silently overwriting one label.

How to check your practice

Explain the example in your own words, inspect input constraints, then verify against the source. Revisit concepts if you cannot explain why; supply missing dates, fields or permissions; withdraw unsupported conclusions.
